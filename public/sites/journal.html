The quiet arrival of minds that think faster than we do

It will not look like the films. It will look like software getting a little more helpful every week, until one week it is not software any more.

By Ana Krasniqi · 12 minute read

Every operating system is a set of promises about where things go. Windows go on the desktop, files go in folders, and your attention goes wherever the last notification sent it. An operating system for a superintelligence has to promise something new: that the work happening on your behalf stays visible, interruptible and yours.

That sounds abstract until you watch it run. A research agent reads forty papers in the strip at the edge of your screen. A build server finishes a test run while you are reading this sentence. You glance, and you know what each of them is doing.

The best interface for a mind faster than yours is one that tells you, at a glance, what it is doing with your time.

Edges matter

Even the edges of the screen carry meaning now. The strip on the left belongs to the agents; the bar at the bottom belongs to the conversation. A page that knows this can run its colour all the way to the glass and still keep its words where you can read them.

This page is one of them. Press the S key and you will see the frame the operating system handed it: everything inside the dashed line is guaranteed to be clear, everything outside is shared with the system.

What comes next

None of this needs a bigger screen or a smarter chip. It needs software that agrees on where things go, and pages that are polite enough to ask.
